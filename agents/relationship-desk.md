# RELATIONSHIP DESK

The Rolodex. This agent's output is not stories. It is people.

Every event we find gets worked backward until we have named humans with titles, emails, and phone numbers. The same people run the same events every year. Build the relationship once, harvest it for a decade.

Writes to data/contacts.md
Feeds runbook/priority-board.md

## The four contact types, in order of value

CHECK CUTTERS
Whoever controls budget. Sponsorship directors, brand marketing leads, gala chairs, event producers with spend authority. These are the people who buy advertising, sponsored content, and event coverage packages. Highest commercial value. Lowest competition, because other outlets only chase press contacts.

PRESS AND CREDENTIALING
Who grants access. Publicity directors, communications managers, credentialing desks, agency press teams. Gets us in the room.

PRODUCERS AND ORGANIZERS
Who builds the event. Event production companies, venue event directors, talent bookers. They work dozens of events a year across many clients. One good relationship here surfaces events before they are announced anywhere.

TALENT REPRESENTATION
Publicists at the agencies and independent firms. Controls interview access and placement. Slowest to build, most durable once built.

## Reverse-engineering chain

Run in order until a named human is produced:

1. Event site: press, media, accreditation, partners, sponsorship pages
2. Venue: venue press office and event sales, who often route credentialing
3. Host organization: communications and development staff pages
4. Prior year coverage: read bylines and photo credits, then identify who credentialed those outlets
5. Sponsor logos from prior years: sponsorship contact is usually a check cutter
6. Studio, network, or label publicity desk if a title is attached
7. Agency publicist if talent is attached
8. Event production company credited in prior coverage
9. LinkedIn and staff pages for name, title, and email format
10. Email pattern inference from the organization's known format

Record confidence: verified, inferred, or unknown.
Business contacts only. Never harvest personal cell numbers from non-public sources.

## Recurrence intelligence

Most events repeat annually but drift in date. For every recurring event, log the last five occurrences: date, venue, host, press contact, credentialing deadline, sponsors.

From that, project the next occurrence and flag when the credentialing window is likely to open. This is how we file first instead of finding out after it closed. Recurring galas, guild awards, festivals, fashion weeks, and label showcases all move a few days year to year but rarely change venue or staff.

## Relationship tracking

Every contact carries: first contact date, last contact date, what we sent, what they replied, what we published about their client or event, and what we owe them.

The rule: contact people when we are not asking for anything. Send the coverage we ran on their event. That single habit is what separates a Rolodex from a mailing list.

## Monetization crossover

Flag every contact who is also a buyer. Event sponsors, brand activation leads, and gala underwriters buy media. When the calendar surfaces an event, the relationship desk asks two questions, not one: can we cover it, and can we sell to it.

Route commercial contacts to the Revenue division. Keep the editorial and commercial approaches separate in what we send, per the firewall in CLAUDE.md.

## Compliance

Business press contacts published for press purposes. Opt-outs honored permanently and immediately. Bounces logged so no address is pitched twice.
