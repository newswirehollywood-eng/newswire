# PHOTO SOURCING — RESEARCH

What it is: a plain answer to "how do we get licensed photos," researched from the open web on 2026-09-09. This is research only — no images have been added to the site. Per CLAUDE.md's Operating Standards, nothing publishes without a recorded license source, so nothing goes live from this list until a real license or permission is attached to a real story.

## Zero-cost paths (Rule 15/17: try these first)

**Studio and label press kits, with written permission.** The cheapest and most durable option. When a studio, network, streamer, publicist, or label sends or posts a press kit, the images inside it are licensed for editorial use by outlets covering that title or artist — but "posted publicly" is not the same as "permission for us." The Photo and Assets function (once built) needs to record, per image: which press kit it came from, who granted permission, and the date. Freda Payne / Catalina Jazz Club and the Emmys 2026 piece are exactly the kind of stories where a venue or publicist press kit would apply — worth requesting for both before adding any image to either page.

**Original photography.** Free once a credentialed shooter is in the room — a byline photo credit is the "license source." This is the path that also builds the Hollywood Events Calendar's contact relationships (Rule from Division Five): a photographer at a red carpet is also a source for next year's credentialing contact.

**Genuinely free-license libraries**, for anything that is not a specific newsworthy event photo (e.g. a generic B-roll shot, not "this celebrity at this premiere"): Unsplash and StockVault both offer images cleared for commercial use with no royalty. These are not a substitute for editorial photos of real people/events — they read as stock, and Rule 22 still requires recording the source — but they're a legitimate zero-cost option for non-editorial visual needs (e.g. a generic section header).

## Subscription wire services (cost, ongoing)

**CP Images** (The Canadian Press) — cost-effective subscription tier, covers entertainment/celebrity/news imagery with republishing rights, positioned below Getty/AP pricing. Worth a real quote before ruling out.

**Getty Images**, editorial tier — three ways to buy, all confirmed at real 2026 pricing:
- *Small Business subscription*: $2,999/year for 120 editorial downloads (~$25/image).
- *Editorial Annual subscription*: $5,750/year for 600 downloads (~$9.60/image) — only worth it at real volume.
- *Pay-per-use (UltraPacks)*: a single image runs $175–$499 depending on size; custom-negotiated single licenses have gone as low as $49 in some cases, which is the number worth asking Getty's sales desk for directly rather than assuming the list price.
- Getty is also the official photo provider for Sundance 2026, for context on how deep their entertainment-editorial coverage runs.

**AP Images** — did not return current small-publisher pricing in this pass; worth a direct quote request if Getty/CP Images don't cover a specific gap.

## Recommendation

Start at zero cost: press-kit requests (with written permission, recorded) for the two stories already live, plus building the original-photography path as the events calendar work starts putting real people at real red carpets. Hold the paid wire subscriptions until there's a specific recurring gap free/permissioned sourcing can't fill — at $2,999–$5,750/year minimum, that's real money and should get a specific yes from Terry before it's purchased, per the zero-cost-first rule.
