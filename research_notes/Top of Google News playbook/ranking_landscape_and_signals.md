# Who Dominates News Search and Why: Publisher Ranking Landscape and Google/Bing News Ranking Signals (late 2025 – Nov 2026)

> Research date: 21 Nov 2026. **Method caveat:** in this session, direct page fetching was blocked for every outside site tried (pressgazette.co.uk, newsdashboard.com, developers.google.com, detailed.com, ppc.land). All findings below come from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Exact figures should be checked on the source page before anyone publishes them. Where search summaries disagreed with each other, this is flagged.

## Q1. Which publishers have the most search visibility / Top Stories / Discover share?

### Takeaway
A small set of big brands takes almost all news-search real estate. In a 2024 study, the top 1,000 domains held 97.3% of Top Stories listings, and 704 of them were established media brands. The New York Times is the #1 US news site by traffic and the most consistent winner of 2026 core updates. CNN leads NewzDash's overall US Top Stories visibility for 2025. In entertainment, People.com, NYT and Page Six led big event coverage (Met Gala 2025). Traffic across the top 50 US news sites fell 37% from July 2024 to July 2026, so even the leaders are shrinking.

### Inferences
- Visibility is concentrated in established national brands (NYT, CNN, Fox, People, Page Six, Guardian). For entertainment, the competition is People, Page Six, NYT, Daily Mail, and the trades (Variety, Deadline, THR) plus TMZ and E!/US Weekly/ET. Exact rank order for the trades and TMZ was not confirmed here.
- Every Top Stories slot an independent site wins is taken from a large brand. The realistic opening is niche or local entertainment topics where the big brands are slow or absent, which topic authority and the Feb 2026 Discover "local" push both favor.
- Because Discover now delivers about two-thirds of Google news traffic, Discover eligibility (large images, non-clickbait headlines, engagement) matters as much as Top Stories.

## Q2. What signals decide who ranks for breaking news? (Google's statements and practitioner evidence)

### Takeaway
Google names relevance, prominence, authoritativeness, freshness, location and original reporting. Its topic authority system rewards outlets whose original reporting other publishers cite on a given beat, and outlets with a strong reputation in that subject or locality. Practitioners add: being fast and crawlable (Google cannot wait to render JavaScript), a clear factual headline that names the entity, consistent topical focus, and accumulated trust. Google says political viewpoint and ad relationships are not ranking factors.

### Cited Findings
**What Google says**
- Google's "How news works" page describes news ranking by relevance, prominence, authoritativeness, freshness, location and language. It says it does not rank by political or ideological viewpoint, and that ad sales or commercial relationships are not ranking factors. — [Google, How news works](https://www.google.ca/search/howsearchworks/how-news-works); [Google News help, How news works on Google](https://support.google.com/googlenews/answer/9311628)
- Topic authority (Google post, May 2023): Google looks at how much a publication's original reporting (for example, the outlet that first broke a story) is cited by other publishers, to judge its influence and authority on a topic. It also counts reputation, such as a history of original reporting or journalism awards. It helps local publishers appear more often for news in their area, and it targets specialized areas like health, politics and finance. — [Google Search Central, Understanding news topic authority](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/05/understanding-news-topic-authority); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-launches-new-topic-authority-system-to-better-surface-news-content-427485)
- Google's Search Liaison said topic authority "isn't a new system that's just launched. We've used it for several years." It does not appear in Google's official ranking-systems guide. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-topic-authority/487603/)
- Transparency signals: Google asks news sources for clear dates, bylines, author information, publisher and contact details. It has described article-level signals (byline links to author bios, "Opinion" or "News" labels) and site-level signals (funding, mission statements). — [Google News policies](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-news-transparency-policy-signals-31519.html); [Google Search Central, sources behind Google News (2021)](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/06/google-news-sources)
- Google's Feb 2026 Discover core update aims to show more locally relevant content from sites based in the user's country, and less sensational content and clickbait. — [Google Search Central blog, Feb 2026 Discover core update](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)

**What practitioners evidence (opinion and experience, not Google confirmation)**
- Barry Adams: speed is "the defining difference" in news SEO. Google renders JavaScript slowly and cannot wait for a full render, or for Chrome Core Web Vitals data, before ranking a breaking article. Articles need to be server-rendered and crawlable at once. — [NewzDash webinar with Barry Adams](https://www.newzdash.com/guide/advanced-tech-seo-news-publishers-barry-adams-webinar); [SEO for Google News, Core Web Vitals](https://www.seoforgooglenews.com/p/core-web-vitals-news-publishers)
- Adams on headlines: the structured-data (NewsArticle) headline is in his view the most important for Top Stories. Google usually shows the H1 as the visible headline. The hard limit is about 110 characters, and Top Stories cuts off at about 70–80. Headlines should be factual and make the topic and entity clear. — [SEO for Google News, The 5 article headlines](https://www.seoforgooglenews.com/p/the-5-seo-article-headlines-google); [Making Headlines for News](https://www.seoforgooglenews.com/p/making-headlines-for-news)
- Adams: news ranking rests more than regular SEO on accumulated authority and trust. Niche sites should use specific main categories matching their beat. Author structured data helps establish author entities. — [SEO for Google News, Structured Data](https://www.seoforgooglenews.com/p/structured-data-for-news-publishers); [Advanced Web Ranking interview](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/blog/news-seo-ai-search)
- Adams: Discover and Top Stories share core quality and relevance algorithms, but Discover leans harder on personalization and engagement signals. — [Advanced Web Ranking](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/blog/news-seo-ai-search)
- NewzDash: a strong Search foundation is a main factor in stable Discover visibility. — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-traffic-drops-news-publishers-40645.html)
- Jessie Willms / SEO for Journalism on whether AI has changed topic authority ("No, but also yes"). Details were not surfaced in the search snippet. — [SEO for Journalism](https://www.seoforjournalism.com/p/has-ai-changed-topic-authority-no)

### Inferences
- For a small entertainment wire, the levers within reach are: publishing fast with server-rendered HTML, a NewsArticle schema headline that leads with the named person or title, a tight beat (for example, LA events and credentialed red-carpet coverage), and original reporting that bigger outlets cite. Citation by other publishers is the core topic-authority signal.
- CTR and links as direct ranking signals are claimed by practitioners but not confirmed by Google. Treat them as plausible, not documented.

### Gaps
- No direct statements from Lily Ray or John Shehata on breaking-news signal weighting were surfaced.
- No 2025–2026 controlled study on the effect of publish speed or headline match was found.

## Q3. 2025–2026 algorithm and policy changes that matter

### Takeaway
Between late 2025 and Nov 2026: a punishing December 2025 core update; the first Discover-only core update (Feb 2026: local and anti-clickbait); March and May 2026 core updates that favored authoritative primary sources over aggregators (NYT and Guardian won); recurring spam updates; Preferred Sources going global; Top Stories appearing inside AI Overviews; and steep falls in search referrals, hitting small publishers hardest (−60% over two years, per Chartbeat).

### Cited Findings
**Core and Discover updates**
- December 2025 core update: 52 of 75 UK news sites lost visibility (Sistrix). The Times and Substack were among the few winners. Some sites reported Discover collapses within 48 hours. — [Press Gazette, Dec 2025 core update](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/times-and-substack-among-few-uk-winners-from-google-december-2025-core-update/); [Search Engine Land, Feb 2026 Discover update](https://searchengineland.com/google-releases-discover-core-update-february-2026-468308)
- February 2026 Discover core update: began 5 Feb 2026 and finished 27 Feb (about 3 weeks). US English first, with other countries and languages to follow. It favors locally relevant content from sites in the user's country and demotes sensationalism and clickbait. RebelMouse said local news sites in its network benefited most. — [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update); [Search Engine Roundtable, rollout done](https://www.seroundtable.com/february-2026-google-discover-core-update-done-41006.html); [RebelMouse](https://www.rebelmouse.com/2026-google-discover-algorithm-update)
- March 2026 core update (finished 8 April): little effect on news overall. The biggest gainers were The Guardian, Money Saving Expert, Substack and NYT. Yahoo Finance and US News & World Report fell. — [Press Gazette, March core update](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/march-google-core-update-brings-modest-gains-for-news-websites/); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/nyt-and-guardian-win-big-as-googles-march-2026-core-update-wraps/)
- May 2026 core update (21 May to 2 June): Sistrix tracked 8,887 domains with 5,039 winners and 3,845 losers at midpoint. Its 77-domain UK news sample was net negative. Analysts (Aleyda Solis, using Sistrix) saw visibility move from aggregators and intermediaries to primary destinations. Sistrix summary: "authority beats interchangeability." **These are secondary sources.** — [triaza.com](https://triaza.com/blog/seo/google-may-2026-core-update/); [postsprout](https://postsprout.app/articles/google-core-update-may-2026-winners-losers-recovery)
- One result mentioned a "November 2026 core update," but nothing corroborated it. **Unverified.**

**Spam updates**
- August 2025 spam update: 27 days in two waves. It hit scaled thin content and aggressive affiliate patterns. Spam updates were reported in March 2026 (completed in about 19.5 hours), June 2026 (no new policy categories) and September 2026 (scaled content abuse among the policies covered). **Reports conflict in detail. Google publishes no target list.** — [RebelMouse](https://www.rebelmouse.com/google-spam-update-2025); [seo-kreativ, March 2026](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-march-2026-spam-update/); [Digital Applied, June 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/google-june-2026-spam-update-rollout-site-owner-guide); [Stan Ventures](https://www.stanventures.com/news/7-google-spam-updates-later-where-seo-stands-in-2026-7573/)

**Preferred Sources**
- Tested in Search Labs in June 2025, launched in the US and India in August 2025, expanded to English-language users worldwide in December 2025, with other languages to follow. Users star a source next to the Top Stories header. Chosen sources appear more often in Top Stories or a "From your sources" section, and can carry a "preferred" badge in AI Mode and AI Overviews. Google provides publishers with a button to add to their site. More than 600,000 sources have been selected. Google has not quantified the ranking effect. — [Google blog, preferred sources](https://blog.google/products/search/preferred-sources/); [Google blog, expansion](https://blog.google/feed/preferred-sources-expands/); [Search Central docs, preferred sources](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-expands-preferred-sources-publisher-ai-partnerships/563062/)

**AI Overviews, AI Mode and click loss**
- Pew (900 US adults, browsing panel): users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary present, against 15% without. Links inside AI summaries were clicked on about 1% of visits. — [Search Engine Journal summary](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/impact-of-ai-overviews-how-publishers-need-to-adapt/556843/)
- Chartbeat: Google Search page views across its network fell 34% (Dec 2024 to Dec 2025) and 40.2% year on year (July 2025 to July 2026, 2026 Publisher Playbook). Over two years, search referrals fell 60% for small publishers (1k–10k daily page views), 47% for mid-sized and 22% for large. — [Axios, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/17/chartbeat-search-traffic-ai-chatbots?stream=top); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/); [Android Headlines, Oct 2026](https://androidheadlines.com/2026/10/google-search-publisher-traffic-declines-40-percent.html)
- Ahrefs: AI Overviews correlate with a 58% drop in CTR for top-ranking pages. SparkToro/Similarweb: 68.01% of Google searches end without a click (up from 60.45% in 2024). Similarweb puts zero-click at 69% of queries. **Methods vary widely, with figures from 22% to 68% for nominally the same metric.** — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-ai-overviews-publisher-links-search-traffic); [Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/ai-overviews/)
- Top Stories now appears inside AI Overviews about 20% of the time (Press Gazette). Entertainment is the news category most likely to trigger a Top Stories box inside AI Overviews: 35% in the US and 31.5% in the UK (NewzDash). — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/google-adds-top-stories-links-to-many-news-related-ai-summaries/); [NewzDash](https://www.newzdash.com/guide/top-stories-ai-overviews-data-strategy-and-impact-blocking-google-ai); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-top-stories-inside-ai-overviews-means-for-publishers-and-brands-in-2026-and-beyond/584175/)
- Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 (June 2026): weekly use of AI chatbots for news rose from 7% (2025) to 10%, with 17% among 18–24s and 5% among 55+. Only 1% say AI is their main news source. Trust in news from AI chatbots is 20%, against 37% for news generally. Publishers forecast a 40% drop in search referrals over three years. **The click-through figure is disputed across write-ups (4% vs 42% "always/often click through").** — [Reuters Institute, AI chatbots chapter](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/emerging-uses-ai-chatbots-news-and-what-it-means-journalism); [GIJN](https://gijn.org/stories/2026-reuters-institute-digital-news-report/); [IFJ](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/category/ai/article/reuters-digital-report-2026-journalisms-pivot-navigating-the-ai-and-creators-squeeze)

**Bing / MSN / Copilot**
- Microsoft's Publisher Content Marketplace licenses content from Business Insider, Condé Nast, Hearst, AP, USA Today and Vox Media for its AI products, with Yahoo among the first demand partners. **Secondary source.** — [capconvert](https://www.capconvert.com/learn/blog/microsoft-ads-in-2026-copilot-integration-publisher-marketplace-and-what-s-new)

### Inferences

- Asking loyal readers to add Newswire Hollywood as a Preferred Source is a free, Google-sanctioned lever.

## Q4. Penalties and what not to do

### Takeaway
The live risks are site reputation abuse (third-party or sponsored sections riding the host's authority), scaled content abuse (mass-produced pages, whether AI- or human-written), expired-domain abuse, Google News policy violations (misleading previews, undisclosed sponsorship, missing transparency), and Discover policy breaches. Repeated or severe violations remove eligibility for News and Discover.

### Cited Findings
- Spam policies introduced March 2024: scaled content abuse (mass-produced, low-value content however it is made: automated, human or mixed), expired domain abuse (buying an old domain to rank low-value content), and site reputation abuse (third-party content exploiting a host site's ranking signals). — [Google Search Central, March 2024 core update and spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- Site reputation abuse enforcement started May 2024 with manual actions. CNN, USA Today, Fortune and the LA Times lost coupon-query visibility. Late 2024 manual actions hit affiliate sections such as CNN Underscored and WSJ Buyside, and Forbes Advisor reportedly had folders deindexed. Some sections were removed days before Black Friday 2024. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-begins-enforcement-of-site-reputation-abuse-policy-with-portions-of-sites-being-delisted-440294); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-strengthens-policy-against-site-reputation-abuse/533018/); [Press Gazette, Black Friday](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/google-dealt-blow-to-publisher-shopping-revenue-on-eve-of-black-friday-site-reputation-abuse-update/)
- In January 2025, enforcement reached Italy, Spain and France, with reported hits including focus.de, bild.de, chip.de, Le Figaro and Le Monde. On 13 Nov 2025 the European Commission opened a Digital Markets Act investigation into whether the policy unfairly demotes publishers that carry commercial partners' content. **Mostly via an aggregator; verify.** — [ppc.land, site reputation abuse](https://ppc.land/site-reputation-abuse/)
- Google News policies: content that disguises sponsored material as editorial is not allowed. Sponsorship (payment or ownership ties) must be clearly disclosed. Previews must not promise what the article does not deliver (the nearest rule to a clickbait ban). Repeated or severe violations cost eligibility for news surfaces. — [Google News policies](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050)
- Discover content policies apply Search's overall, spam and feature-specific policies. Repeated or severe violations make a site ineligible for Discover. — [Discover content policies](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/9982767)
- Feb 2026 Discover update explicitly demotes sensational content and clickbait. — [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)

### Inferences
- Our "PAID OUT" (sponsored client) content must carry a visible label. If the volume becomes large, or it covers topics far from our news beat, it risks being treated as site reputation abuse. Keeping sponsored work clearly labeled and on-beat (or noindexed or on a separate section) reduces the risk.
- Wire cards that only link out, with no republished text, are lower risk than scraped republication. But a site that is mostly aggregation is the profile the 2026 core updates demoted.
- Bulk AI-written pages are covered by scaled content abuse whether or not a human touches them.

### Gaps
- No documented case of manual actions on small entertainment news sites in 2025–2026 was found.
- The outcome of the EU DMA investigation as of Nov 2026 was not found.

## Q5. How a new small publisher earns Top Stories / Google News eligibility

### Takeaway
No application is needed. Since December 2019, Google has automatically considered all publishers for Top Stories and the News tab, provided they follow Google News content policies. Publisher Center is optional and does not affect eligibility. Eligibility is not ranking: the Search Console News performance report shows whether you actually appear.

### Cited Findings
- "Publishers are automatically considered for Top stories or the News tab of Search." — [Google Publisher Center Help, News content across Google](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9607025?hl=en)
- Google automatically crawls the web to include sites in Google News: "There's no application needed!" Publisher Center is "entirely optional" and does not affect eligibility. — [Google News Initiative, Get started with Publisher Center](https://newsinitiative.withgoogle.com/resources/trainings/start-building-your-audience/get-started-on-google-news-with-publisher-center/)
- The switch to fully automated inclusion came with the December 2019 Publisher Center launch. All sites are eligible, but not all content will appear. The Search Console News performance report is the way to check. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-all-sites-are-eligible-to-be-in-google-news-but-not-all-content-will-appear-in-google-news-350521)
- AMP is not required. AMP and non-AMP pages are both eligible if they meet page experience and content policy requirements. **Secondary source.** — [seopine](https://www.seopine.com/blog/google-news-seo-top-stories)
- A 2025–2026 Search Engine Land guide covers launching a news publication that earns Google visibility. Contents were not read. — [Search Engine Land, How to launch a news publication](https://searchengineland.com/news-publication-google-visibility-484268)

### Inferences
- The path for Newswire Hollywood: follow the News policies (bylines, author pages, dates, about and contact pages, labeled sponsored content); use NewsArticle structured data; publish fast, crawlable HTML; build a citable original-reporting record on a defined LA entertainment beat; add the Preferred Sources button; then watch the Search Console News report.
