Peter Rice takes charge of Netflix's content this week. Here is what he inherits

The former Disney television chief became Netflix's chief content officer on October 26, replacing Bela Bajaria and taking charge of a programming budget of roughly $20 billion, in the same month the company cut about 1,000 jobs.

By the Deals Desk, Newswire Hollywood  |  Oct 29, 2026  |  Los Angeles

Peter Rice started this week as the most powerful programming executive in streaming. Netflix named him chief content officer on October 15, effective Monday, October 26. He replaces Bela Bajaria, who had held the top creative job since January 2023 and had been with the company since 2016.

The job is the whole slate. Rice oversees Netflix's global content organization: scripted and unscripted series, film, and live events, according to CNBC. TheWrap puts the budget that comes with it at roughly $20 billion a year.

Who he is

Rice is a studio and network operator by trade. He ran Fox Searchlight and later held senior roles at 21st Century Fox. After Disney bought the Fox entertainment assets, he ran Disney's general entertainment content, overseeing its cable channels, news programming and live sports coverage, until Disney fired him in June 2022, as Deadline recalled in its report on the appointment. Before Netflix, he was head of ceremonies and content for the LA28 Olympics.

Co-CEO Ted Sarandos called him one of the most effective at-scale operators in the industry.

Why now

The change arrived with a cut. Bajaria's exit came as Netflix eliminated about 1,000 jobs, roughly 6% of its workforce, The Hollywood Reporter reported. Analysts quoted by TheWrap read the move as a back-to-basics turn toward film and television, after slowing engagement and new pressure from a Skydance-controlled Paramount-Warner Bros.

For the town, the practical question is simple. Every agent, showrunner and producer with a project in Netflix's pipeline now has a new top decision-maker, and a new boss's first months are when slates get reviewed. Newswire Hollywood will report the first greenlights and the first cancellations made on Rice's watch.
