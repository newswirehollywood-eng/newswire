Taylor Swift extends her Video of the Year record, Madonna wins seven and takes Artist of the Year

Taylor Swift won Video of the Year for a sixth time, a record nobody else is near. Madonna returned to the stage after twenty-three years and walked off with seven. Snoop Dogg hosted from the Peacock Theater.

By the Music Desk, Newswire Hollywood  |  Sept 28, 2026  |  Updated Sept 29  |  Los Angeles

Madonna opened the 2026 MTV Video Music Awards at the Peacock Theater in downtown Los Angeles on Sunday night — her first performance at the ceremony in twenty-three years — and left it the evening's biggest winner, with seven trophies including Artist of the Year for Confessions II.

The night's single largest prize went elsewhere. Taylor Swift took Video of the Year for “The Fate of Ophelia,” extending a record that is already hers alone. It is her sixth in the category, after “Bad Blood” in 2015, “You Need to Calm Down” in 2019, “All Too Well: The Short Film” in 2022, “Anti-Hero” in 2023 and “Fortnight” in 2024. She thanked Dolly Parton from the stage.

Madonna had arrived as the most-nominated artist of the evening with thirteen, a career high, across Video of the Year, Artist of the Year and Song of the Year. Snoop Dogg hosted.

Snoop Dogg hosted.

The carpet

Arrivals ran long and ran loud. Taylor Swift, Charli XCX, Tyla, Adam Lambert and Troye Sivan worked the line. Chuck D and Flavor Flav came through together. Lisa Rinna arrived in an ivory gown with a sculpted bust, a thigh-high slit and fabric draped off one shoulder, the train doing most of the talking.

The winners

Video of the Year — Taylor Swift, “The Fate of Ophelia.” Her sixth, and a record only she holds.

Artist of the Year — Madonna, for Confessions II, the sequel to her 2005 album. Seven awards in all, more than anyone else on the night.

Best Director — Taylor Swift, “Opalite.”

Best Pop — LISA, “Dream,” featuring Kentaro Sakaguchi.

Best Hip-Hop — Cardi B featuring Kehlani, “Safe.”

Artist Director Honors — Taylor Swift, the inaugural recipient of a category created this year, which MTV describes as recognising work that builds interconnected narratives, cinematic worlds and recurring details across a body of work. Swift premiered the video for “Patient Zero” during the broadcast. She came in with eleven nominations.

Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award — Nirvana.

What the night was actually about

The VMAs have spent a decade being written about as a show looking for a reason to exist. The answer this year was not a format change. It was Madonna on the stage that built her, twenty-three years on, walking off with seven — not a tribute, a sweep.

But the record that moved belongs to Taylor Swift, who now has six Video of the Year wins and no one else within reach. Two eras of the same show, holding the two biggest prizes on the same night.

Set that beside a posthumous Vanguard for Nirvana, a Dolly Parton tribute from Kacey Musgraves, and a George Michael tribute from Raye, sombr and Teddy Swims, and it was a night that spent much of its running time looking backward. Whether that reads as reverence or as hedging is the thing worth arguing about this morning.
